There's a particular magic in returning to a book you loved as a child. It's like finding an old sweater in the back of the closet—it might be a little worn, the sleeves might be slightly too short, but the warmth is immediate and entirely familiar.

I spent this past weekend curled up in the corner chair of the shop, rain drumming against the front window, rereading a fantasy novel I hadn't touched in fifteen years. What struck me wasn't just how well the story held up, but how the book served as a time machine. I wasn't just reading the story; I was remembering the person I was when I first read it.

> "A book read by a ten-year-old is a different book when read by a thirty-year-old, even if the words haven't changed."

As bookstore owners, we spend so much time looking forward—new releases, upcoming catalogs, the next big debut. But there is profound value in looking backward. The worn paperbacks on our shelves aren't just inventory; they are anchors. They remind us why we fell in love with this quiet, profound act of staring at marked paper and hallucinating vibrant worlds.

So this is your gentle nudge: go find that book. The one with the cracked spine and the tea-stained cover. Spend an afternoon with an old friend. You might be surprised by what they have to say to you now.
