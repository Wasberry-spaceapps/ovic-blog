People often ask me for my "favorite book." It's an impossible question. As a bookseller, picking a favorite book is like asking a baker to pick a favorite grain of flour. 

But if you ask me which books *changed* me? That’s a list I can write. These are five books that, once finished, left me looking at the world with new eyes.

### 1. The House on the Cerulean Sea
This book taught me that softness is not weakness. In a literary landscape often dominated by grit and darkness, this story proved that radical kindness and boundless empathy can carry a narrative just as powerfully as a sword fight.

### 2. Braiding Sweetgrass
A quiet revolution of a book. It completely rewired how I look at the natural world, shifting my perspective from seeing the earth as a resource to seeing it as a community of teachers. I haven't looked at a tree the same way since.

### 3. A Wizard of Earthsea
The magic system here is based on knowing the *true name* of things. It’s a profound metaphor for understanding and attention. It taught me that to have power in the world is simply to be willing to look closely at it and call it what it is.

### 4. Station Eleven
This story asks a vital question: *What is worth saving when everything falls apart?* The answer it gives—art, music, connection, memory—reminded me why I sell books in the first place. Survival is insufficient.

### 5. The Phantom Tollbooth
I read this as a child, and it was the first time I realized that words could be played with. It showed me that language isn't just a tool for communication; it’s a toy box. 

What are the books that shifted your world? The next time you're in the shop, I'd love to hear about them.
