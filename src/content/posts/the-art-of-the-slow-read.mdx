At the end of every year, social media fills up with reading recaps. *I read 100 books this year!* *I finished 52 novels!* 

It’s wonderful to celebrate reading, but sometimes I worry we’ve turned it into a competitive sport. We track pages, we set aggressive goals, and we skim over gorgeous prose just to add another notch to our digital belts.

Lately, I’ve been practicing the art of the slow read. 

What does a slow read look like? It means putting the book down after a particularly beautiful sentence just to let it ring in the air for a moment. It means reading one chapter before bed and resisting the urge to binge the next three. It means noticing the texture of the paper, the choice of the font, the rhythm of the author’s voice.

> "A book is not a marathon to be won; it is a landscape to be walked."

Last month, I took four entire weeks to read a single, rather short novel. I carried it with me everywhere. It got a little battered in my bag. I read it while waiting for the kettle to boil, sitting on the bus, and during quiet moments at the counter. Because I spent so much time with it, the characters began to feel like actual fixtures in my daily life.

When we rush through books, we consume them. When we read them slowly, we inhabit them. Next time you pick up a book, try giving yourself permission to just... meander. The finish line isn't going anywhere.
